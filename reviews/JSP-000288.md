# JSP-000288 review

Review only. This branch does not add a Lean formalization, does not change the catalog, and does not claim priority.

- Problem: JSP-000288, Erdős problem 346, as stated by Bloom at https://www.erdosproblems.com/346 and in `problems/catalog-0201-0300.md`.
- Catalog question: must consecutive ratios in a minimal stably complete sequence with a uniform ratio gap converge to the golden ratio φ = (1 + √5) / 2?
- Recorded mathematical answer: no. Credit remains GPT Pro, prompted by Liam Price (counterexample). Graham's sequence `a_n = F_n - (-1)^n` is a positive instance whose ratios tend to φ. It is not the counterexample. The counterexample is the irregular perturbed sequence.
- This review's conclusion: the public prose does not state one lemma precisely enough to finish an independent kernel-checked proof in this run. No Lean files were added. `Lean proof` stays **No**.

## What the construction is

The published answer on https://www.erdosproblems.com/346, Liam Price's forum note of 19 June 2026 (https://www.erdosproblems.com/forum/thread/346), and the source card at https://erdosproblems.ai/wiki/library/additive_bases/price_2026_counterexample_erdos_problem_346 describe the same counterexample.

GPT Pro, posted by Liam Price, constructs a strictly increasing sequence of positive integers such that:

- deleting any finite subsequence leaves a complete sequence (every sufficiently large integer is a sum of distinct remaining terms);
- deleting any infinite subsequence leaves an incomplete sequence;
- `a_{n+1} / a_n ≥ 6/5` for every n;
- along an increasing index sequence `N_j`, `a_{N_j} / a_{N_j-1} → φ` and `a_{N_j+1} / a_{N_j} → φ + 1/4`.

The two subsequential limits are different, so the consecutive ratios do not converge, and in particular they do not converge to φ. That is the negation of the catalog claim.

The five-page preprint is the Overleaf read link https://www.overleaf.com/read/tgrrgqpbjpht#c5a085, cited from `data/sources.yaml` in https://github.com/plby/lean-proofs. The wiki card, updated 8 October 2026, summarizes it as follows and says the proofs were read for structure only:

- Lemma 2. A sequence that eventually satisfies Graham's recurrence `x_{n+2} = x_{n+1} + x_n - (-1)^n` has every tail complete, and both `x_{n+1}/x_n` and the normalized tail sums tend to φ.
- Admissible sequences obey `a_{n+1} = S_{n-1} + b_n` with `0 ≤ b_n ≤ a_n/4`.
- Lemma 3. Deleting any infinite subsequence of an admissible sequence leaves it incomplete.
- Lemma 4. A finite interval certificate keeps a tail complete under every later admissible choice.
- Lemma 5. A long enough unperturbed stretch produces such a certificate.
- The counterexample sets `b_{N_j} = ⌊a_{N_j}/4⌋` at a sparse index sequence `N_j`.

Nat Sothanaphan's forum note of 20 June 2026 says the Lean corresponds to the paper and that the printed proof of Lemma 3 has a typo: the bound should be `j ≥ 4`, not `j ≥ 3`. The note does not write the corrected argument.

The Overleaf token page did not return the paper. Direct download URLs for the PDF and the project zip responded 404 on 16 October 2026. The wiki card states that it holds no file of the source. There is no proof exposition on the problem page.

## The lemma that is missing

**Lemma 4, the permanent interval certificate, is not stated.**

The prose names the certificate and says that once a tail represents a sufficiently long interval, every later admissible perturbation preserves completeness. It does not give:

- the endpoints of the represented interval;
- the numerical lock that relates that length to the prefix sum and to the admissible bound `b_n ≤ a_n/4`;
- the length of the unperturbed stretch that Lemma 5 needs in order to produce the certificate;
- the sparsity rule that chooses `N_j` after the certificate so that both ratio limits hold at once.

Without that lock, finite-deletion completeness of the perturbed sequence is not a theorem that can be finished from the public prose. Filling it in from the existing Lean development would be a transcription of that source. Inventing a substitute inequality would not be the published argument. Either choice is outside this run, so no Lean project was added and nothing here depends on `sorry` or a custom axiom.

Lemma 3's corrected counting bound is a second gap in the prose. The forum records the typo and not the repaired estimate.
